# Engineering field guide for Trencho V4

Research and application by the Lead Developer, 31 October 2026. This guide supports [team workflow](team_workflow.md), [foundation decisions](foundation.md) and the [current system map](c30_system_map.md). It does not change gameplay, engine version or delivery acceptance. Dawson requested deeper Godot, software-engineering and game-engineering research after repeated integration mistakes. The lead owns applying the findings to assignments and reviews.

## How to use this research

Read the working method and relevant topic before changing a shared boundary. Follow the source links when the engine behavior or tradeoff is unfamiliar. The sources below are official engine references, original design papers, an engineering book published by its authors, an original game-pattern book, and production accounts by the developers of Factorio. They have different evidential strength: an API reference specifies engine behavior; a paper supplies a reasoning tool; a production account demonstrates an experience in another game. None establishes that a pattern will make Trencho faster or correct.

Godot's linked `stable` documentation is a moving reference, consulted on 31 October 2026. Trencho remains pinned to **Godot 4.7.2 standard**. Check version-specific APIs against that executable/source and a focused remote reproduction when a change depends on them. The lead prepared this guide through source review and existing remote evidence, without a new local engine run. Older design literature is used for durable principles, not current API or hardware claims.

The recommendations marked **Trencho application** are our engineering judgments. Retain them only while they reduce a demonstrated source of error or cost. A small explicit method or enum is often the full implementation of a useful pattern.

## The working method

Before assigning substantive work, the lead writes one short slice plan in the owning issue or PR:

1. **Observable outcome:** the ordinary player action and resulting behavior, including what must still happen autonomously. Name the exact starting session and finish condition.
2. **Whole path:** current caller, authoritative owner, downstream consumers, identities and clock. Trace prerequisites from existing finite stock/people to consumption; inspect related callers before adding another policy.
3. **Transition contract:** admission conditions, claims made, physical completion, denial, interruption, cancellation/retry, and who releases each claim. State the conservation and exclusivity invariants.
4. **Engine and cost assumptions:** lifetime, shared data/copies, initialization and pause behavior; expected frequency/data size, potential main-thread blocking, test time/resource limits. Research unfamiliar assumptions first.
5. **Smallest useful proof:** focused failure case, related integration case, ordinary controls/visual observation, and remaining experience/performance gates. Name an independent reviewer for substantive lead-authored shared behavior.

This is usually a few paragraphs and a small table, not a separate design phase. Developers challenge a missing edge before implementing it. The lead remains responsible for the connected result after child tasks finish. Review a runnable increment promptly; avoid accumulating unrelated features behind an unobserved integration.

Google's engineering book treats review as correctness, knowledge sharing and maintainability work; its continuous-delivery discussion connects small batches to useful, timely feedback. **Trencho application:** use one meaningful peer review for shared risks, retain required repository checks, and size slices around a working player sequence. Do not import Google's organization-wide approval machinery or numerical test ratios. [Code Review](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch09.html), [Continuous Delivery](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch24.html).

## 1. Own decisions and state, not just files

Parnas's original modularity paper compares decompositions of the same system. Its useful criterion is to hide a design decision behind a module, particularly one likely to change; dividing a processing flow into files alone does not provide that protection. **Trencho application:** the ledger owns identity/physical accounting; transport owns commitments and travel lifecycle; production owns recipe/work/output lifecycle. A panel or scenario may request work but must not independently implement those rules. [Parnas, 1972, original paper mirrored by the University of Mississippi](https://john.cs.olemiss.edu/~hcc/csci555/notes/localcopy/Parnas_Criteria_Decomposing.pdf).

Godot recommends focused scenes with external dependencies supplied by their composing context. Its scene-tree guidance ties parent/child placement to lifetime, and distinguishes initiating behavior through methods/callables from reporting events through signals. **Trencho application:** keep the session's owners alive while views change; inject the same service into panels and diagnostics. Prefer an explicit command when the caller needs an admission result. Use notifications for interested views after the owner commits. Neither a global event bus nor an autoload is necessary merely because two systems communicate. [Scene organization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/best_practices/scene_organization.html).

Cockburn's original ports-and-adapters article separates application behavior from the devices that drive it, allowing users and automated drivers to use the same application boundary. **Trencho application:** a remote driver should issue the real haul, production, crew, dispatch and supply commands. It can omit mouse movement for a functional test, but cannot replace a missing haul with a ledger grant and call the result ordinary gameplay. Keep separate UI-input evidence. Existing public service methods can supply these boundaries without an adapter framework. [Cockburn, 2005](https://alistair.cockburn.us/hexagonal-architecture).

For each changed rule, ask: who decides, who records the authoritative result, who observes it, and which caller can bypass it? Duplicate **policy** is especially costly. The refuel bug used `Layout.path` for admission while execution used `_walk_from` to exit the production bay. Reusing the existing authoritative helper fixed the disagreement. Similar-looking code is not automatically duplication: different holder, visibility, geometry or duty semantics may justify separate implementations.

## 2. Know Godot's lifetime and data semantics

Choose the lightest engine type that provides the required behavior. Nodes participate in the scene tree; `RefCounted` suits many service/data objects; `Resource` adds engine serialization and Inspector support. **Trencho application:** retain existing plain services unless tree processing, input, transforms or presentation justify a Node. Recipe definitions may benefit from Resources when authoring requires it; migrating all dictionaries to Resources would need a concrete benefit. [Godot node alternatives](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/best_practices/node_alternatives.html).

Loaded Resources are normally shared, including resources referenced by multiple scene instances. **Trencho application:** distinguish authored recipe/configuration data from per-session mutable progress. Never assume loading a resource again creates a new independent inventory or recipe state. Decide whether to share an immutable definition or explicitly create independent mutable state. [Godot Resources](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/resources.html).

Dictionaries are reference types. A shallow duplicate shares nested containers; `duplicate(true)` recursively copies arrays/dictionaries but still shares Resources. Making a dictionary read-only does not recursively freeze its nested contents. **Trencho application:** inspection results should contain detached facts with a deliberate schema; do not expose live mutable service rows to presentation. Do not apply deep duplication indiscriminately in a hot path. Check what actually needs detaching, including object references. [Dictionary reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_dictionary.html).

Reference counting does not collect reference cycles. **Trencho application:** the session can own services strongly while a service uses a weak reference back to its owner, as existing services do. Use strong references for real ownership; use weak references only when the dependent object can cope with expiration. A `RefCounted` label alone is not evidence of leak freedom. [RefCounted reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_refcounted.html).

Parents enter the tree before children; children become ready before their parents. Freeing a Node frees its children. **Trencho application:** avoid a child expecting parent `_ready()` to have injected a dependency already. Supply it before adding the child, or use an explicit bind/activation boundary. Treat runtime lifetime, scene serialization ownership and physical ledger holder as separate concepts; changing one does not transfer goods. [Node reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_node.html).

Autoloads outlive ordinary scene changes; Godot does not enforce a unique instance merely because a script is autoloaded. **Trencho application:** use one only for a justified application-wide lifetime. A session's ledger and battlefield services are not application-global just because multiple panels need them. Preserve the existing explicit session composition. [Singletons / Autoload](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/singletons_autoload.html).

## 3. Make transitions and physical obligations explicit

Meyer's contracts describe caller obligations, supplier guarantees and invariants; they also caution against indiscriminate redundant checks. **Trencho application:** validate public requests against the authoritative current state, return a useful denial, and keep internal invariants explicit. Expected gameplay denials such as an occupied parking lane are normal results, not engine crashes. A preview is advice about the state it read, never permission to skip validation when the command executes. Assertions are useful for impossible internal conditions, not the only validation for player input. [Meyer, Applying Design by Contract, 1992, author's ETH copy](https://se.inf.ethz.ch/~meyer/publications/computer/contract.pdf).

Use a transition table when sequences matter. Describe the state, trigger, guard, resources claimed/released and observable completion. A reservation is not a transfer; a completed production batch is not a delivered shipment; HQ receipt is not a remote weapon's loaded rack. At a synchronous admission boundary, either commit every required claim or release every own active claim, publish no job/commitment and leave physical goods/people unchanged. Retain cancellation/audit history and sequence gaps rather than pretending the rejected attempt never existed. After physical movement begins, cancellation must account for the actual current holder, carried goods and people rather than pretending time reversed.

Nystrom's State chapter explains both the value of explicit states and the combinatorial cost of mixing independent dimensions. **Trencho application:** an enum/table in an existing owner may be enough. Keep vehicle movement, crew duty and ammunition facts distinct, with explicit guards between them. Do not replace all military decision-making with one giant FSM or create a class per trivial state. [State](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/state.html).

The multi-portion ammunition fix illustrates the right abstraction boundary: the player requests a type and quantity; the owner resolves real stock portions, capacities and per-source claims. Preserve identity/origin through each carry and deposit. On a failed admission, roll back every claim, including a partially reserved final source. On success, credit loaded/delivered quantities only after the physical event. This application uses ordinary ownership and transaction reasoning; it does not require introducing an event-sourcing or distributed-transaction system.

Review sequences as well as happy paths: request twice; deny while busy; cancel before and after a carry; lose a handler/crew member; change availability after preview; finish and repeat using the same worker; stop work with cargo still in transit. Select cases that exercise real risks, not every theoretical combination. A batch passing once does not prove repeat operation.

Check **liveness** alongside conservation: safe accounting can coexist with a permanently stuck job. The current forward-transport owner supports interruption/resume with the same handler/task/carry and revalidated route, but has no public cancel/reassign command. Handler death, a dropped carry or changed exact source claims can leave a commitment without a supported recovery path. These are source-reviewed recovery limits, not observed C30 failures or permission to silently reassign cargo. Name the supported recovery and owner; raise a missing gameplay recovery rule with the designer before promising it to the player.

## 4. Respect the game loop and the host computer

Godot's `_process` follows rendered frames; `_physics_process` runs at a fixed physics rate as far as the engine can maintain it. **Trencho application:** use the existing shared simulation clock for logistical event time, and rendering callbacks for presentation. Do not independently advance the same session from rear and battlefield views. Moving a costly arbitrary simulation loop into `_physics_process` does not remove its cost or make it deterministic. [Idle and physics processing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/idle_and_physics_processing.html).

Godot pause behavior depends on process mode, and connected signals can still invoke methods while their receiver is not processing. **Trencho application:** hiding a panel is not enough to stop its refresh queries; changing view must not pause the world accidentally. Verify the actual clock and each view's invalidation behavior. Keep pause/unpause and useful inspection responsive, including after an explicit action at unchanged simulation time. [Pausing games and process mode](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/pausing_games.html).

Nystrom's loop discussion separates simulation progression from rendering and warns against unbounded catch-up that can lock up the application. **Trencho application:** record simulated seconds versus elapsed wall time as well as frame latency. Any future incremental planner needs a bounded work unit, stable ordering, cancellation, stale-result validation and explicit semantics while work is pending. That is a scoped design task if measurement justifies it, not permission to drop simulation rules or silently skip events. [Game Loop](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/game-loop.html).

Fiedler explains how timestep size affects numerical integration, and how expensive catch-up work can make a simulation fall progressively behind. **Trencho application:** keep headroom and measure clock progress; do not copy a fixed-step physics recipe over Trencho's event-boundary simulation without analyzing its semantics. A time clamp, catch-up limit or interpolation is a behavioral choice requiring evidence, not an automatic cure for a blocking service call. [Fix Your Timestep!](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/).

Godot recommends finding CPU bottlenecks with a profiler; profiling itself adds overhead. Inspect inclusive time, self time and call counts, then measure the whole affected operation without the profiler. **Trencho application:** retain exact revision, scenario, engine, renderer, population/time window, speed and instrumentation. Report p95/p99/max frame times and stall counts; an average can conceal an unusable pause. Distinguish simulation, presentation, engine waits, memory growth and total process use. Engine memory counters are not Windows working-set measurements. [CPU optimization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/cpu_optimization.html).

Our full-ledger snapshots, repeated recursive mass inspection and hidden-panel refreshes demonstrated that **data requested and frequency** can dominate cost. Prefer a narrow current query for one needed fact before caching a world snapshot. Cache derived results only with named dependencies, invalidation and freshness guarantees. A per-operation aggregation can avoid long-lived invalidation complexity. Profile the next bottleneck after each meaningful change; do not assume the original hotspot still dominates.

The Dirty Flag pattern defers recalculation until needed, but missed invalidation yields stale results and deferred work can create a visible stall. **Trencho application:** it fits coalesced view refreshes when every relevant action/visibility change is accounted for. It must not suppress physical simulation or rely solely on time changing while paused commands can change state. [Dirty Flag](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/dirty-flag.html).

Data Locality concerns layout and access order when memory behavior is the bottleneck. **Trencho application:** reducing irrelevant reads is warranted by our measurements; a wholesale ECS/structure-of-arrays rewrite is not established by them. GDScript collections do not automatically inherit the layout benefits of C++ examples. A language/plugin change needs measured remaining cost, integration and maintenance evidence. [Data Locality](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/data-locality.html).

Threads are not a general freeze cure. Godot documents restrictions on the active scene tree and shared resources; work must cross a deliberate synchronization boundary. **Trencho application:** any future worker computes from a detached input, and the authoritative owner validates/commits the result. `call_deferred` schedules work; it does not by itself move computation to another CPU. Never send live mutable ledger state to a worker as a shortcut. [Thread-safe APIs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/thread_safe_apis.html), [Using multiple threads](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_multiple_threads.html).

Wube reported parallel update work becoming slower through cache contention, and later a determinism defect exposed by different core counts. It also describes bounding construction-job work and then improving its search algorithm. **Trencho application:** prefer measured algorithm/data-flow improvements before parallelism; if bounded work or threads become necessary, include ordering, backpressure and result ownership in the design. These Factorio results are instructive examples, not predicted Trencho speedups or a requirement for multiplayer determinism. [FFF215](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-215), [FFF415](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-415).

Current resource rule: broad suites, long encounters, stress and profiling run on GitHub. The active C30 batch permits **no further local engine/native attempts** under the designer's direction. A later local trial needs the lead's coordinated bounded resource plan and the current gameplay authority's go-ahead. Preserve the human editor and unrelated applications. The existing 750 ms emergency stop threshold is not a smoothness target. Required checks passing does not settle responsiveness.

## 5. Use patterns selectively

| Problem | Smallest useful mechanism | Tradeoff / avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scene depends on an unknown sibling/global | Inject the existing owner/reference from composition | Do not add a container framework just to pass two dependencies |
| Several callers disagree on an admission rule | One owner command with shared policy used by preview/execution | Revalidate mutable facts at commit; do not confuse DRY with merging different policies |
| Illegal or forgotten lifecycle edges | Explicit state/transition table, guards and focused sequence tests | Avoid one state for every combination of independent facts |
| Several independent views react to a committed change | Local signal / Observer; coalesce refresh | A synchronous handler still blocks; notification storms can cost more than a narrow read |
| Work really must be deferred or aggregated | Owner-local bounded queue with pending/result/overflow rules | Do not add a global event bus for ordinary command/response |
| View repeats expensive unchanged derived work | Narrow query, then dirty/versioned derived view if measured | Every dependency must invalidate; watch the first refresh stall |
| One class mixes independently changing capabilities | Extract the specific cohesive service/component | Wiring, indirection and lifetime add cost; no blanket ECS migration |

Nystrom distinguishes Observer's synchronous notification from an Event Queue's time decoupling, and warns that a global queue retains global-state and stale-world hazards. His Component chapter explains both reuse benefits and extra wiring/indirection. **Trencho application:** keep direct dependencies visible when understanding both sides is necessary; use a queue only when timing separation is required. [Observer](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/observer.html), [Event Queue](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/event-queue.html), [Component](https://gameprogrammingpatterns.com/component.html).

## 6. Prove the right claim at the right scope

The Google testing chapters distinguish test resources from behavioral scope, advise public-behavior assertions, and explain fidelity gaps when dependencies or realistic configuration are absent. **Trencho application:** focused regression protects a defect; a connected ordinary session proves connections; UI interaction proves controls; observation proves visible behavior; resource/frame evidence supports responsiveness. None substitutes for the others. No fixed assertion count or coverage percentage is delivery acceptance. [Unit Testing](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch12.html), [Larger Testing](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch14.html).

| Claim | Useful evidence | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A refuel denial/carry lifecycle is correct | Same real worker completes two carries; busy/oversized/inaccessible cases conserve stock and claims | The entire manufacture-to-battle sequence is playable |
| A connected supply path works | Ordinary start, finite inputs, real commands/holders, stage journal through actual consumption | Controls are discoverable or smooth on Windows |
| A UI action works | Rendered input reaches the intended owner; state/result stays visible | All physical prerequisites or long-term pacing |
| An optimization preserves behavior | Independent retained oracle plus edge/representative cases and invariant checks | A microbenchmark wins in the actual game |
| The game stays responsive | Bounded rendered frame/clock measurements; later coordinated OS input and process-resource evidence | Long-session/full-map hardware certification from a startup screenshot |

For remote observations, record the tested SHA, starting configuration, stage timestamps, quantities/origins, state/denial at failure and a useful ordinary view. Save partial evidence before a timeout. Fail fast when a prerequisite is broken; later errors from that failed prerequisite are not independent root causes. Keep full required CI checks separate from optional observations so a skipped job cannot stand in for validation.

The initial C30 observation never hauled factory ingredients. Its failure was a driver omission, not evidence that manufacturing was broken. The next observation found a genuine repeat-refuel policy disagreement and a legitimate parking denial. These are three different diagnoses. Inspect the owner state and player path before changing production logic or weakening geometry.

The isolated scalar-route optimization was held after signed-zero fixture-premise failures and ambiguous whole-route gains after host controls. Runtime construction/canonicalization was a hypothesis, not a proved serializer defect. Validate unusual fixture inputs actually possess the intended bits/types before testing the guard. Preserve the independent baseline and required parity; compare the whole operation using representative cases and controlled host/workload conditions. Reject a batch whose correctness or practical benefit is unresolved.

## Applying this on the next slice

Start with the earliest unpassed stage in the current [C30 system map](c30_system_map.md). The lead and supporting developer trace its authoritative owner/callers, agree the smallest fix or clarify a legitimate gameplay constraint, and update the short slice plan. Run the relevant focused remote check and one corrected integrated observation when warranted. Inspect retained evidence before asking the human to try the build. Continue the same session toward a second real resupply cycle and meaningful battlefield consequence; do not certify those from the first manufactured hull or first shot.

Existing useful boundaries and tests should be preserved. Architecture work earns its place when it removes demonstrated coupling, repeated policy errors or measured blocking cost in that delivery. Record a lasting boundary decision in `foundation.md`; leave changing run status in the owning issue. The guide is a learning reference, not another approval board.
